# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Fellow makers and crafters who recognize the references (knitting, ceramics, woodwork, DIY), curious general readers with no craft background who are drawn in by voice and warmth, and the author herself: it is a personal log first, a public blog second. Users arrive looking for process honesty, not polish or authority. They want to feel like they are reading over the shoulder of someone who actually makes things and writes about it as-is — sawdust, dropped stitches, and all.

## Product Purpose

Handmade Logic is a personal crafts journal. Its purpose is to document the honest, imperfect experience of making things by hand — not as a how-to resource or a brand, but as a living record. Success looks like readers feeling at home, returning because the voice is familiar, and occasionally discovering something they want to try. The site does not need to perform expertise; it needs to feel genuinely made.

## Brand Personality

Warm, quirky, honest.

Voice is intimate and self-deprecating without being self-effacing. It does not explain itself. It assumes the reader is curious and kind. Tone is like a message from a friend who makes too many things at once and finds that funny rather than embarrassing.

## Anti-references

- **Minimal tech blog**: dark mode, monospace fonts, developer-cool aesthetics. Wrong temperature entirely.
- **Corporate lifestyle brand**: polished wellness or DTC craft brand look. Too slick — loses the handmade soul and reads as aspirational rather than real.
- **Maximalist scrapbook chaos**: every element competing for attention, no breathing room. The site should feel textured and layered, not visually overwhelming. Handmade does not mean unprincipled.
- Implicit anti-reference from the existing code direction: no Pinterest softness, no Etsy flatlay beige-and-white minimalism, no stock-photo warmth.

## Design Principles

1. **Honest over polished.** Imperfection is the point. Rough edges, handwritten touches, and unfinished corners are features. Never sand them away in pursuit of a slick finish.
2. **Texture without noise.** The layered aesthetic (dotted backgrounds, watercolor blobs, SVG illustrations) should create depth, not competition. Every decorative element earns its place by contributing to the feel, not the noise level.
3. **Voice-forward.** Typography and layout exist to serve the writing. Hierarchy should pull readers through the text, not distract them from it.
4. **Warmth is structural.** Color, spacing, and motion choices should all point toward the same physical sensation: sitting next to someone who makes things, in good light, with a warm drink nearby.
5. **Respectful motion.** Animations exist to delight and orient, not to perform. Full reduced-motion support is non-negotiable — the site should be equally readable and pleasant without any motion.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA. Reduced-motion support is already implemented in the CSS and should be maintained on every new animated element. Color contrast should meet AA at minimum across all text and interactive elements — the warm beige background requires careful checking against the lighter palette values. Semantic HTML, keyboard navigation, and visible focus styles on all interactive elements.
