It started, as most of my projects do, with complete confidence and zero preparation.

I'd watched exactly one YouTube tutorial. I had the yarn. I had needles that were *probably* the right size. I had a pattern I'd found on Ravelry that was labelled "beginner-friendly," which I now understand to be a legally unenforceable claim.

## The gauge swatch incident

Every knitting resource ever written tells you to swatch. Knit a small square first. Measure it. Adjust your needle size. Only then proceed.

I did not swatch.

<Quip side="left">I KNEW I should have swatched. I looked right at the instructions and chose chaos.</Quip>

Here is what happened when I did not swatch: I knit the entire back panel of what was supposed to be a fitted crew-neck in a size medium, only to hold it up and discover I had created something that would fit a very cosy refrigerator.

The frogging (that's knitting-speak for unraveling — "rip it, rip it") took forty minutes. The crying took slightly less.

## What I actually learned

After the third restart, something clicked. Not in a mystical way. In a very boring, practical way that nobody makes YouTube thumbnails about:

**Tension is everything, and tension is you.**

My stitches were tight when I was stressed — commute, deadline, slightly too much coffee — and loose when I was relaxed. The fabric was a physical diary of my week. I found this either poetic or infuriating depending on the day.

<Quip side="right">the scarf basically has a chapter about that one Tuesday where everything went wrong</Quip>

I also learned kitchener stitch, which is a method for grafting live stitches together invisibly. It requires you to follow a four-step mantra (*knit off, purl on, purl off, knit on*) while also holding two needles, managing yarn tension, and not dropping anything. It is genuinely one of the more satisfying things I have ever done, and I will not be explaining why.

## The scarf it became

Look. The proportions weren't right for a sweater. But I had this beautiful length of knitted fabric in a deep burgundy merino, and I wasn't going to frog it a fourth time.

It's a scarf now. A very good scarf. It has excellent drape. Several people have asked where I got it, and I have said "I made it" in a tone that omits the preceding chaos.
